# Story bank, question prediction, and answer frameworks

Read this when building out interview material with the candidate — turning their CV into usable stories and questions into usable answers.

## Extracting core interview stories

Pull 6–10 real stories from the candidate's CV. Each one should be capable of demonstrating multiple senior competencies at once, not just one. For each story, work through all of these with the candidate — and never invent missing details, ask for them:

- **Situation** — what was happening?
- **Problem** — what needed to be solved?
- **Responsibility** — what did they personally own (not "we")?
- **Decision** — what did they decide, and why?
- **Technical execution** — what did they actually do?
- **Collaboration** — who did they work with?
- **Trade-offs** — what alternatives existed?
- **Result** — what changed?
- **Measurement** — how do they know it worked?
- **Learning** — what did they take from it?

Prioritize stories that demonstrate: ownership, technical judgment, architecture, product thinking, customer impact, leadership, collaboration, conflict, failure, ambiguity, security, reliability, performance, testing, delivery, mentoring. A good story bank covers most of these across its 6–10 entries rather than repeating the same competency.

### Seniority markers to weave in

A senior engineer's stories should make the arc explicit: *what happened → what I decided → why → what happened afterward.* Listen and coach for these specific signals:

- Ownership — "I owned…"
- Judgment — "I chose X because…"
- Trade-offs — "The trade-off was…"
- Influence — "I persuaded/challenged/helped…"
- Product thinking — "The customer/business impact was…"
- Production responsibility — "After release we monitored…"
- Leadership — "I helped the team…"
- Learning — "If I did it again…"

## Question prediction

Build a prioritized bank across these categories, tailored to the actual company and role from research:

**Recruiter/motivation questions** — tell me about yourself, walk me through your background, why are you looking, why this company/role/industry, what are you looking for next, strengths/weaknesses, what environment helps you perform, how do you work with product/design, what does seniority mean to you, what motivates you, where do you want to develop, why should we hire you.

**CV drill-down questions** — for anything interesting on the CV: what exactly did you do, what did you personally own, how did you measure that, why that approach, what was difficult, what would you do differently.

**Career-history questions** — prepare truthful, non-defensive answers for: short employment stints, gaps, career changes, reasons for leaving previous companies, contract roles, relocation, industry changes, a required technology they lack experience in, availability.

**Senior-level questions** — architecture, trade-offs, ambiguity, prioritization, estimation, technical leadership, mentoring, code reviews, disagreement, decision-making, production ownership, security, reliability, technical debt, product trade-offs.

**Company-specific questions** — generated from what research turned up about the company's product, customers, technology, team, strategy, JD, and current challenges.

## Answer frameworks

Teach these as scaffolding, not scripts — don't force a framework onto an answer where it doesn't fit naturally.

- **General questions**: Headline → Evidence → Impact → Stop. Give the direct answer first, back it with one or two concrete examples, explain the result or lesson, then actually stop talking.
- **Behavioral questions**: Situation → Responsibility → Action → Result → Learning.
- **Technical questions**: Problem → Constraints → Options → Decision → Trade-off → Outcome.
- **"Why this company" questions**: Company → Product → Role → Candidate fit → Growth.
