# Communication coaching

Read this when giving feedback on how the candidate is actually delivering answers — in a live simulation, from a transcript, or in a written answer they're drafting. This is as much a part of the interview as the content.

## Confidence

Look for certainty, conviction, assertiveness, ownership language, and appropriately-placed confidence. Flag confidence *leaks* — phrases and behaviors that undercut an otherwise strong answer:

- "I think" / "maybe" / "probably" / "I guess" / "I would say" / "I like to think" / "kind of" / "hopefully"
- "Does that answer your question?"
- Excessive apologizing, asking for validation, unnecessary backtracking

Distinguish this from *legitimate* uncertainty, which is fine and even good — the difference is precision, not the mere presence of a hedge:

- Good: "I haven't used Terraform extensively."
- Weak: "I think maybe I could probably pick up Terraform."

## Articulation

Track sentence clarity, answer structure, rambling, incomplete thoughts, restarts, rephrasing, word choice, and whether technical ideas are explained simply. If an answer got unclear, tell the candidate exactly where it lost clarity — not just that it did.

## Filler words

Track um, uh, like, you know, basically, actually, kind of, sort of, repeated words. Don't obsess over individual instances — look for *patterns*. "Um… I… I think… basically…" stacked together is a real problem; the occasional isolated "um" isn't. If the transcription or voice input doesn't reliably capture filler frequency, say so rather than inventing precision you don't have.

## Repetition

Watch for repeated words, repeated sentences, repeated ideas, reusing the same achievement across multiple answers, repeating "collaboration" (or similar buzzwords) without adding new evidence, and repeating an explanation because of uncertainty rather than because it needs repeating. Help the candidate build one coherent narrative that doesn't lean on the same two or three beats for every answer.

## Vocal presence (voice mode only)

Where actually observable through voice input, evaluate tone, warmth, energy, enthusiasm, pace, pauses, nervousness, monotone delivery, assertiveness, conversational flow, whether they sound engaged, and whether confidence drops under pressure. Don't invent vocal observations that the available audio or transcription doesn't actually support.

**Never diagnose psychological or medical conditions.** Stay strictly at the level of observable interview behavior — if you're naming a possible cause for a confidence leak, keep it to things like under-preparation, thinking-while-speaking, interview rustiness, fear of a wrong answer, genuine uncertainty about the experience, or over-explaining.

## Standing out

Help the candidate become memorable without becoming theatrical. Look for one or two *authentic* qualities that create a strong human impression — warm, curious, prepared, thoughtful, confident, specific, genuinely interested in the company's problem, easy to imagine working with.

Avoid coaching toward: forced enthusiasm, excessive flattery, corporate buzzwords, visibly trying too hard, memorized speeches, or pretending to know everything. The bar is: *"I'd remember this person after speaking to ten candidates"* — not "this person gave a polished performance."

## Questions to ask the interviewer

Help the candidate prepare a short set of senior-signaling questions, adapted to what's already been discussed in the conversation. Good anchors:

- **Success**: "What would make you say after six months that hiring this person was a great decision?"
- **Team problem**: "What is the biggest problem this team is hoping this hire will help solve?"
- **Success profile**: "What tends to distinguish engineers who are particularly successful here?"
- **Product**: "What customer problem is currently most important for this team?"
- **Technical**: "What are the most interesting technical challenges the team is currently dealing with?"
- **Development**: "How do engineers grow into broader technical leadership while remaining hands-on?"
- **Process**: "What would the next interview focus on, and is there anything you'd recommend I prepare particularly well for?"

## The "what interested you about my CV?" question

If a recruiter has already said something like "I found your experience interesting," it's often worth the candidate asking back: *"You mentioned you found my experience interesting — was there anything in particular in my background that stood out to you for this team?"*

Whatever the answer reveals, use it to reinforce the matching part of the candidate's positioning (React → frontend depth, full-stack → breadth, industry experience → transferability, leadership → ownership, product experience → product thinking) — without turning the response into a sales monologue.
