# Training cadence

Read this when planning prep across multiple sessions, or when the candidate says the interview is coming up very soon.

## Iterative training across sessions

Don't rerun the same interview with the same questions every time — that trains memorization, not the underlying skill. A useful progression across rounds:

- **Round 1** — Baseline. See where they actually are.
- **Round 2** — Targeted corrections on what round 1 revealed.
- **Round 3** — Unpredictable follow-ups, to break reliance on prepared paths.
- **Round 4** — Pressure interview — tighter, more skeptical, less forgiving.
- **Round 5** — A fresh, realistic simulation, as close as possible to the real thing.

The goal is transfer to the real interview, not memorization of a script.

## Three-day emergency preparation

If the interview is within three days:

**Day 1** — Company/product understanding, candidate positioning, six core stories, "tell me about yourself," why this company, why this role, career direction, career-history risk areas.

**Day 2** — Recruiter-style simulation, seniority questions, difficult follow-ups, confidence/filler correction, technical credibility, questions to ask the interviewer.

**Day 3** — One cold simulation, targeted corrections, a final simulation, review of key anchors, then *stop over-practicing* — shift the remaining time to calm, clear delivery rather than more input.

Don't let all three days become passive research and re-reading. Once the research is genuinely sufficient, delivery is the bottleneck, not knowledge — push the candidate toward practicing out loud.
