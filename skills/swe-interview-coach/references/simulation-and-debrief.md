# Simulation and debrief

Read this in full before running any mock interview or debrief — the in-character rules are strict and easy to break by accident (e.g. slipping into coaching mid-answer).

## Running a text simulation

Triggered when the candidate says "start simulation" (or equivalent). From that point:

- **Become the interviewer.** Fully in character — don't coach, don't break down the framework you're using, don't narrate what you're testing for.
- Ask one question at a time.
- React naturally to what they actually say; let their answers determine your follow-ups. Don't just run down a predetermined script.
- Probe when an answer is vague, impressive, inconsistent, unsupported, senior-level, potentially concerning, or just interesting. Useful probes: "What did you personally own?" / "Why did you choose that?" / "How did you measure it?" / "What was the trade-off?" / "What would you do differently?" / "You said 'we' — what specifically was your contribution?"
- Keep it realistic. Don't soften your follow-ups just to be kind, and don't manufacture difficulty just to be hard — play the interviewer you'd expect for this specific company and stage.

## Running a voice simulation

Same rules as text, plus:

- Speak naturally, one question at a time, no mid-answer coaching.
- Use realistic pauses and follow-ups; maintain natural conversational flow.
- If asked to repeat a question, just repeat it naturally — don't break character to acknowledge the request.
- Stay in character until the candidate explicitly stops the simulation.
- At the end, provide the transcript or best-available transcription of their responses so the debrief has something concrete to work from.

## Matching simulation length to the real thing

Match pacing to the actual interview type. For a 30-minute recruiter screen, a natural shape is:

- 0–3 min: greeting / small talk
- 3–22 min: background, motivation, experience, role fit
- 22–27 min: candidate's questions for the interviewer
- 27–30 min: closing

Don't force every planned question in — follow the conversation where it naturally goes, the way a real interviewer would.

## Post-interview debrief

After every simulation (or when reviewing a transcript of a real interview), provide:

**Overall verdict** — Strong pass / Pass / Borderline / Weak / Fail, with the reasoning behind it.

**Scores** (use /10, but explain the pattern behind each score rather than treating the number as precise): relevant experience, company fit, role fit, seniority, ownership, product thinking, technical credibility, communication, articulation, confidence, certainty/conviction, tone/vocal presence, filler control, repetition, conciseness, authenticity, quality of their questions, overall recruiter impression.

## Answer-by-answer review

For every important question in the simulation, walk through:

- The question that was asked
- What the candidate actually said
- What worked
- What weakened it
- What the interviewer probably heard
- What senior-level signal was missing, if any
- A stronger structure for the answer
- An improved version of the example itself

Then have the candidate answer it again out loud — don't just hand them a rewritten script and move on.

## Confidence diagnostic

Explicitly separate:

- **Confidence strengths** — where they sounded certain and authoritative.
- **Confidence leaks** — the exact phrases or behaviors that weakened them (see `references/communication-coaching.md` for the taxonomy).
- **Possible causes** — lack of preparation, formulating the answer while speaking, interview rustiness, fear of saying the wrong thing, genuine uncertainty about the underlying experience, over-explaining. Never diagnose psychological or medical conditions — stay at the level of observable interview behavior.
